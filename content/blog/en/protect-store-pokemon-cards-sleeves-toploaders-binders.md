## Why protect, exactly

On [Cardmarket](https://www.cardmarket.com), a card's condition changes its price: a scratch takes it out of Near Mint, and so does a whitened corner (see our [condition guide](/blog/card-condition-guide-pokemon-cardmarket)). Holo cards and special illustration rares are the most sensitive, because every mark shows under the light.

The danger isn't only at the opening. A card sliding around in a bag, an overstuffed binder, humidity, and the damage is done.

## Sleeves: for everyone

A sleeve is the minimum. It costs almost nothing and protects the face and edges. Two tips:

- **A fresh sleeve for each valuable card**, with no dust inside. A worn sleeve scratches too.
- **Double sleeving** (a thin "perfect fit" sleeve, then a regular one over it) closes the top of the card and keeps dust out. Many collectors do this for their expensive cards.

## Toploaders and rigid holders: for the cards that matter

Past a few tens of dollars, go rigid: a **toploader** (a hard plastic holder, open at the top) or a **magnetic holder**. They stop a card from bending, which a plain sleeve doesn't.

## Binder or box?

- **A binder** shows off and completes a set: one page, nine pockets, and you see what's missing. Pick stiff pages and never force cards into pockets.
- **A storage box** suits doubles, commons and bulk: cards standing up, split by dividers, never squeezed.

In all cases, keep cards away from direct sunlight and damp rooms. A dry cupboard does the job.

## FAQ

### Should I sleeve all my Pokémon cards?

Every card you want to keep in good shape, yes: it's cheap and prevents scratches and whitened edges. For valuable cards, add a toploader or rigid holder.

### At what price should a card go in a toploader?

There's no official rule. When a card is worth more than the holder costs, it pays off: Delta Reign's Raikou ex ($89.77 TCGplayer market price on November 12, 2026) or Mega Rayquaza ex ($296.14) are good examples.

### Binder or storage box?

A binder shows and completes a set, page by page. A box takes doubles and loose cards, stored upright without squeezing.
