> **In short**
>
> - Cardmarket grades cards on **7 levels**: Mint, Near Mint (NM), Excellent (EX), Good (GD), Light Played (LP), Played (PL) and Poor (PO).
> - **Whitened, inked or blackened borders mean Poor**, however good the rest of the card looks. Scratches cap a card at Good at best.
> - A card's "trend" price doesn't say which condition it is: always compare the cheapest offer with the trend **and** with the listed condition.

On Cardmarket, two copies of the same card can sit at very different prices without anyone cheating. The reason is three letters: NM, EX, LP. Here is how to read them before you hit "Buy".

## The 7 Cardmarket conditions

From best to worst: **Mint**, **Near Mint**, **Excellent**, **Good**, **Light Played**, **Played** and **Poor**. Cardmarket doesn't support partial grades ("NM-", "EX+") and recommends not using them. There is no "Gem Mint" either: a seller can write it in the comment, not in the condition.

One thing trips people up: the European scale has 7 levels, the US shops' scale 5. Cardmarket's "Excellent" isn't everyday-English "excellent", and Cardmarket itself admits that one person may call a card Near Mint while the next calls it Excellent. Keep a margin: the grade is the seller's opinion.

## What knocks a card down a grade (or six)

The rules on Cardmarket's help page are stricter than most people think:

- **Clouding** (visible cloudiness on a foil's background): the card can no longer be Near Mint.
- **Scratches**: Good at best, mostly a foil problem.
- **Whitened borders**: the card must be listed as Poor.
- **Inked or blackened borders**: always Poor, and selling a doctored card as Mint is fraud.
- **Water damage**: Light Played to Poor depending on the damage. A card warped enough to be illegal in tournaments is Played.
- **Age doesn't matter**: "no allowance is made for the card being old".

Edge whitening is the most common flaw on cards pulled from packs, and on Cardmarket it is enough to make a card Poor.

## What is the card worth in each condition?

Cardmarket publishes **one "trend" price per card**, built from sales of every condition and language mixed. English cards on NookDex are priced on TCGplayer instead (US market, dollars). The most valuable card of [Pitch Black](/en/sets/pitch-black), Mega Darkrai ex Special Illustration Rare (no. 116), has a TCGplayer market price of $136.89 and a lowest listing of $124.90 (TCGplayer, October 22, 2026). The gap between the two is a reminder that prices depend on the listing's condition, language and seller.

NookDex's trade mat applies a house estimate: −15% for an EX card down to −70% for a PO card. It's a rule of thumb, not a market price. On a card worth over $100, always look at the seller's photos.

## Buying without regrets

1. **Filter by condition**: Near Mint at least for a collection piece, EX if you play with sleeved cards.
2. **Ask for photos** on any card over $20: edges, corners, surface under light.
3. **Check the seller**: their rating and number of sales say more than the listed grade.
4. **Don't compare** a "Mint" offer with an "LP" one: that's the price of the condition, not of the card.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between Near Mint and Excellent on Cardmarket?

Near Mint is a practically new card; Excellent allows a few small visible flaws. Cardmarket says the line is subjective, and visible clouding on a foil rules out Near Mint.

### Can a card with whitened borders be sold as Near Mint?

No. Under Cardmarket's rules, a card with whitened borders has to be listed as Poor.

### Which condition does the Cardmarket trend price correspond to?

None in particular: it is calculated from sales of all conditions together. Mega Darkrai ex (Pitch Black, no. 116) sits at $136.89 on TCGplayer on October 22, 2026, with the cheapest listing at $124.90.
